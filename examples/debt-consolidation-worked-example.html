Worked Example

Combining two debts into one loan: is it actually cheaper?

A fully worked example using the current 7.20% consolidation rate · Calculated 30 July 2026

Consolidation adverts promise one smaller payment. Sometimes that genuinely costs you less; often it quietly costs you more, because the smaller payment comes from a longer term rather than a cheaper rate. Here is the arithmetic, worked through honestly for a typical situation.

The starting position

Meet a borrower with two debts (illustrative figures — the consolidation rate below is a real, current rate):

  • A personal loan: €7,000 left at 12.00% APR with 36 months to run — €230.45 a month, €1,296.36 of interest still to pay.
  • A credit card: €3,000 at 22.9% APR (the current representative APR on the Avant Money One Card), paying a fixed €150 a month — cleared in 25 months, costing €715.65 in interest.

Do nothing, and the pair costs €2,012.01 in remaining interest at a combined €380.45 a month.

Three scenarios, one table

The consolidation option is a €10,000 personal loan at 7.20% — the best standard bank rate on our comparison table today (PTSB). The only decision left is the term, and it is the term that decides whether this works.

The verdict, and the catch

Consolidating over 36 months wins clearly. It saves €901 in interest and cuts the monthly outlay too (€308.65 against €380.45), because the expensive card debt stops compounding at 22.9% and starts costing 7.20% instead. Matching the new term to the old loan's remaining 36 months means no hidden stretch.

The 60-month version is the trap dressed as relief. Its €197.88 monthly payment looks far friendlier, but €762 of the €901 saving evaporates into the extra two years of interest. You'd still come out slightly ahead of doing nothing — by €139 — but you'd also be in debt for two years longer, with all the risk that carries.

The honest summary: consolidation in this example is worth doing because the card debt is expensive and the term stays short. Change either condition and the case weakens fast. Run your own numbers before deciding — our loan comparison shows every current consolidation-eligible rate.
