Reading the gaps

The pattern in that table is the single most useful thing to know before applying anywhere.

With banded pricing (PTSB), the advertised 7.20% is nearly the whole story: even at PTSB's ceiling of 8.80% APR you'd pay €426 more over five years. The rate depends on how much you borrow, not on your credit score, so the advert is a reliable predictor of your offer.

With risk-based pricing (Revolut, Avant Money), the advertised number is the shop window, not the shelf price. Revolut's own published range runs from 6.50% to 12.99% APR — a €1,743 spread on this loan. Avant's runs wider still: an applicant offered the 19.9% maximum pays €255.50 a month instead of €203.65, and €3,111 more in total. That is the difference between Avant being one of the cheaper loans on the market and one of the dearest.

None of this makes risk-priced lenders a bad choice — a strong credit profile can land the bottom of the range, which beats most banks. It means the comparison you should make is conditional: if you expect a clean credit check, the advertised rates compare fairly; if not, weigh the top of each range too. Either way, get the actual quote before committing — a quote, unlike an advert, is personal.
