DIRT: 33% tax on savings interest

Most savings interest in Ireland is taxed at 33% under DIRT (Deposit Interest Retention Tax). Irish banks and some EU providers (e.g. Revolut, MoCo) deduct it automatically. Many EU platforms pay interest gross, so you must declare and pay it yourself via Revenue. An Post/NTMA State Savings products are the exception — entirely tax-free. Over-65s on a low income may qualify for a DIRT exemption via Form DE1.

Deposit protection

Every EU-licensed bank is covered by a national Deposit Guarantee Scheme (DGS) protecting up to €100,000 per person, per institution — the same limit whether the bank is Irish or based elsewhere in the EU. Marketplaces like Raisin spread your money across separate partner banks, so you can hold €100,000 protected at each one. State Savings carry a full Irish sovereign guarantee instead of DGS cover.

How this comparison works

Accounts are grouped by type — instant access, regular saver, notice, fixed term, State Savings, and credit unions — because they aren't directly comparable on rate alone. Within each group we rank by AER, highest first. Figures are gathered from provider websites and independent Irish savings-rate trackers; rates change frequently, so always confirm the current rate and full terms directly with the provider before opening an account.
