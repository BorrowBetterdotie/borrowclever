Credit Union vs Bank Loan in Ireland: Which Is Cheaper? (2026)

Rates, membership, speed and flexibility compared · Rates verified 22 July 2026 · Examples based on €10,000 over 5 years

Credit unions and banks lend money in fundamentally different ways. A bank sells you a product; a credit union lends to you as a member-owner. That shapes who can borrow, how fast the money arrives, how your application is judged — and what you pay. On price, bank and credit union rates are closer than you might expect, but they're not equal, and the answer depends heavily on which credit union you can access. This guide puts real, current Irish rates side by side, then walks through the other differences so you can decide which route fits you.

The short answer: the cheapest standard bank loan (PTSB, 7.20% APR) beats the average credit union (10.42% APR) by about €860 on €10,000 over 5 years. A well-priced credit union can beat some banks — but only a personal quote from your own credit union will tell you whether yours does.

Why the credit union average doesn't tell the full story

The 10.42% ILCU average is a blunt instrument. It is derived from a survey of affiliated credit unions carried out in July/August 2025 — a snapshot of the national picture, not your credit union's current rate.

Individual credit union rates vary significantly by:

  • Which credit union you belong to. Some charge well below the national average; a small number charge close to the 12.68% APR legal maximum.
  • Loan amount. Many credit unions price by band. First Choice charges 8.84% on €15,000–€25,000, for example, but a smaller loan from the same credit union can carry a higher rate.
  • Your savings record. Credit unions take your history of saving with them into account. A member with consistent savings over years will often be offered a better rate than a recent joiner.
  • Loan purpose. Some credit unions offer preferential rates on certain loan types (car loans, home improvement, etc.).

The practical implication: the average tells you roughly where the market sits, but you cannot use it to predict what your own credit union will offer you. You need to ask them directly.

When a credit union can beat a bank

At the best credit union rate we've confirmed — First Choice Credit Union at 8.84% APR — a credit union is cheaper than AIB's standard 8.95% APR personal loan. The difference on €10,000 over 5 years is about €30 in total interest.

That's a real saving, but a modest one — and it only holds if your credit union's rate is below the bank you're comparing against. Against PTSB's 7.20%, the same credit union would cost about €440 more. The more compelling case for credit unions tends to be on smaller loans, where bank minimum amounts (€5,000 at An Post Money and Avant Money, for example) can leave a gap that credit unions fill, and for members whose savings record earns them a better-than-standard rate.

Note: the three cheapest products in the table above — the SBCI green loans at 2.95%–4.20% — are bank products, not credit union products. They require an approved SEAI home energy grant and that 75% of the loan goes to qualifying energy upgrade works. If you qualify, no credit union can match them. Access: the common bond is the first hurdle

The single biggest practical difference is that you cannot simply walk into a credit union and borrow. Irish credit unions operate on a common bond — membership is tied to where you live, work, or in some cases worship or study, and each credit union defines its own bond. Before a loan is possible you need to:

  1. Find a credit union whose bond you fall under — check ilcu.ie.
  2. Open a share (savings) account and make contributions — most credit unions want to see you save with them for a period before you borrow.
  3. Apply once you're an established member.

A bank or fintech, by contrast, will lend to any eligible applicant with no prior relationship. If you need money this week, that difference usually decides the question on its own.

Speed: banks win when time matters

Banks and fintechs can approve and pay out a personal loan within days. AIB advertises a 3-hour decision for existing customers. Revolut's loan is app-only with a near-instant decision. Because a credit union loan generally requires membership plus a savings record first, it is rarely the fast option — it rewards planning ahead, not urgency.

Assessment: the savings-record advantage

Most Irish credit unions check the Central Credit Register like any other lender — but they tend to take a broader view of individual circumstances, and they give real weight to your savings history with the credit union itself. A member who has saved steadily for years will often be offered better terms than a stranger with the same credit file. If your credit record has bumps but your savings habit is strong, a credit union may look at you more sympathetically than a bank's criteria allow.

Flexibility and extras on each side

Bank product features

  • Bank of Ireland lets you defer your first 3 repayments on its personal loan.
  • An Post Money charges no early repayment fee, offers terms up to 120 months, and drops to 6.90% APR on loans of €20,000+.
  • PTSB lends from €1,000 to €75,000 over up to 84 months.
  • Revolut prices by credit score — its 8.41% is an average across a 6.5%–12.99% range, so your own quote may be higher or lower.

Credit union member benefits

  • Possible dividends. Some credit unions pay an annual dividend on members' savings from their surplus. It is not guaranteed — the board votes on it each year, sets its own rate, and it can be zero — so there is no single dividend rate for credit unions as a group. The share of Irish credit unions paying a dividend at all has risen sharply (roughly 18% in 2023 to around three-quarters in 2025). Where one is paid, it indirectly reduces the net cost of borrowing; check your own credit union's latest AGM notice or annual report for its actual rate. Banks offer nothing comparable.
  • Member-owned structure. A credit union answers to its members, not shareholders — surplus goes back into the institution and its community.
  • Preferential rates on some loan types. Some credit unions price car loans or home-improvement loans below their standard rate.

How to choose: a simple decision path

  1. Need the money within days? Bank or fintech. A credit union loan isn't built for urgency unless you're already an established member.
  2. Qualify for a green loan? If you have an approved SEAI grant, the SBCI energy upgrade loans (from 2.95% APR) beat everything else in the market — see the SEAI green loan eligibility guide →
  3. Already a credit union member with a savings record? Get their quote first — it may beat several banks, and the relationship counts in your favour.
  4. Neither? Start from the cheapest standard bank rates on our comparison table, then ask a credit union you're eligible to join whether it can do better.

Our verdict

For most standard personal loans, the best bank rates (PTSB at 7.20%, Bank of Ireland at 8.30%) are cheaper than the credit union national average (10.42%), and banks are faster and open to anyone. But if you're a member of a well-priced credit union — or can join one — you may find a rate that beats some bank products, particularly if you have a strong savings history with them.
