How Balance Transfer Credit Cards Work in Ireland

The mechanics, the fine print, and the exit plan — before you pick an offer

A balance transfer is the one tool that lets you carry credit card debt in Ireland without paying interest on it — for a while. You move a balance from your current card to a new card with an introductory offer, pay it down at 0% (or a reduced rate), and clear it before the offer expires. Done right, it's the cheapest debt in the country. Done wrong, you're back at 22.9% APR with a second card in your wallet. The mechanics, step by step

  1. Apply for a card with a balance-transfer intro offer. You apply to a new issuer — the balance must come from a card at a different institution.
  2. Request the transfer. The new issuer pays off your old card and adds that amount to your new card's balance. Most Irish offers require this within a set window — typically 90 days of opening.
  3. Pay it down during the intro period. Your transferred balance sits at the introductory rate — 0% on most offers — for the set number of months.
  4. Clear it before the revert date. Whatever remains when the offer ends is charged at the card's standard rate.

The two kinds of offer in the Irish market

True 0% offers

Most Irish balance-transfer offers are genuine 0% for a set period — currently ranging from 6 months (Revolut, PTSB Ice) through 7 months (Bank of Ireland's cards), 9 months (Avant Money One Card), up to 12 months (An Post Money Classic — Ireland's longest window).

Reduced-rate offers

Two cards instead offer a reduced rate for 12 months: Bank of Ireland Affinity at 2.9% fixed and AIB Platinum at 3.83% variable (capped at €5,000 transferred). Not free — but far below standard card rates, and the longer window can beat a shorter 0% offer on a balance too large to clear in 6–9 months.

The fine print that decides whether it works

  • The transfer window. Miss the deadline (typically 90 days from opening) and you lose the introductory rate entirely. Initiate the transfer as soon as the card opens.
  • The same-issuer rule. You cannot transfer between two cards from the same bank — an AIB balance can't move to another AIB card.
  • New-customer conditions. Bank of Ireland's 0% offers are for new customers only — an existing BOI cardholder won't qualify.
  • Either/or offers. Some BOI cards make you choose between the 0% balance-transfer offer and a 0% purchase offer — you can't have both.
  • Balance caps. AIB Platinum caps transfers at €5,000; anything above sits at the standard rate.
  • One transfer per card. PTSB Ice allows a single balance transfer — you can't top it up later.
  • Minimum payments still apply. Missing one can cancel your introductory rate. Set up a direct debit for at least the minimum on day one.
  • Spending on the card is a separate question. New purchases may be charged at a different rate than your transferred balance, depending on the card's terms. The cleanest approach: use the transfer card for the old debt only, and don't spend on it.

What it costs even at 0%

"0%" means zero interest, not zero cost:

  • €30 government stamp duty applies to every Irish credit card every year — including your new transfer card.
  • Possible transfer fees. Some issuers charge a percentage of the amount transferred. Fee structures vary — confirm directly with the issuer before applying.
  • Annual card fees on a small number of cards (BOI Platinum Advantage ~€76.18/year, BOI Aer ~€78/year).

Against interest at 16.8%–22.9% APR, these costs are small — but factor them in when the balance is modest.

The revert rate: where the trap springs

Every intro offer ends, and the remaining balance then reverts to the card's standard rate — 16.8% to 22.9% APR on most Irish cards. The issuers' business model assumes a share of customers won't clear the balance in time. Beat it with an exit plan set on day one:

  1. Divide the balance by the offer length. €3,000 on a 12-month 0% card means €250/month clears it at zero interest cost.
  2. Set the direct debit above the minimum — at the clearing amount if you can.
  3. Diary a checkpoint two months before the revert date. Still carrying a balance? Either clear it, switch to another issuer's 0% offer (resetting the clock), or move to the lowest standard-APR card if you'll carry debt long-term — see representative APR explained → for why the standard rate then becomes the number that matters.

When a balance transfer is the wrong tool

A transfer suits card debt you can clear within the offer window. For larger balances, debt spread across several products, or repayment horizons of years rather than months, a fixed-term personal loan at 7–9% APR usually beats cycling between cards — it forces the debt to shrink on a schedule.
