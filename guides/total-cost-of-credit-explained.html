Total Cost of Credit Explained: What Borrowing Really Costs

Why the headline rate isn't the price — and how the same €10,000 can cost €757 or €3,346 in interest

Lenders advertise rates. But you don't pay a rate — you pay euros. The total cost of credit is what borrowing actually costs you: every euro you repay beyond the amount you borrowed. It's the number we rank every loan by on our comparison table, and once you understand it, a lot of loan marketing loses its power. This guide explains how it works, using the same calculation method we use across the site.

The definition, with a worked example

Total cost of credit = total repayable − amount borrowed.

Take the cheapest standard personal loan in the Irish market: PTSB at 7.20% APR. Borrow €10,000 over 5 years and you repay approximately:

  • €197.88 per month for 60 months
  • ≈ €11,873 total repayable
  • ≈ €1,873 total cost of credit — the price of the loan

That €1,873 is the number to compare, because it collapses rate, term, and compounding into a single euro figure you can weigh against alternatives.

APR vs the nominal rate: which one is "the" rate?

Loan paperwork shows two rates, and they do different jobs:

  • The nominal interest rate is the raw rate used to calculate your monthly repayment.
  • The APR (annual percentage rate) is the standardised all-in comparison figure — the total cost of the credit expressed as an annual rate.

Because monthly repayments derive from the nominal rate while APR is the comparison metric, two loans' monthly costs may not track their APRs exactly. When you're choosing between lenders, compare APRs (or better, total cost of credit) — never the nominal rates in isolation.

What the rate does to the bill

Same €10,000, same 5-year term, different APRs — calculated with the standard compound interest formula we use across the site:

APR Monthly (approx.) Total cost of credit (approx.) Where you'd see this rate
2.95% €179.29 €757 Cheapest SBCI green loan (SEAI grant required)
7.20% €197.88 €1,873 Best standard bank rate (PTSB)
10.42% €212.24 €2,734 Credit union national average (ILCU)
12.68% €222.43 €3,346 Credit union legal maximum

From cheapest to most expensive, the same €10,000 costs over four times as much in interest. This is why qualifying for a conditional product like an SBCI green loan — or being a member of a well-priced credit union — matters far more than any promotional feature. The term effect: a lower monthly payment is not a cheaper loan

The trap most borrowers fall into isn't the rate — it's the term. Stretch the same loan over more years and the monthly repayment falls, but the total cost rises. €10,000 at 8.30% APR (Bank of Ireland's standard personal loan rate):

Term Monthly (approx.) Total cost of credit (approx.)
3 years €313.36 €1,281
5 years €202.76 €2,166
7 years €155.86 €3,092

Going from 3 years to 7 years cuts the monthly bill in half — and more than doubles the interest. When a lender offers you a longer term "to make it affordable", this is what it costs. Choose the shortest term whose monthly repayment you can genuinely sustain.

Three practical ways to cut your total cost of credit

  1. Shop the APR first. The gap between the cheapest and dearest standard rates in the market is worth hundreds of euros on a typical loan. Start from the full comparison table →
  2. Take the shortest term you can afford. As above — the term moves the total cost as much as the rate does.
  3. Check the early-repayment terms. If you may clear the loan ahead of schedule, a lender with no early repayment fee (An Post Money, for example) lets you cut the total cost later. Confirm the current terms with the lender before applying.

Why credit cards don't have a total cost of credit

A credit card has no fixed term and no fixed balance, so there's no "total repayable" to calculate. That's why our credit card comparison ranks cards by representative APR instead — the standardised metric every Irish issuer must publish, based on a €1,500 balance repaid over 12 months including the €30 government stamp duty.
