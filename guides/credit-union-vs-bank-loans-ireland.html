Credit Union vs Bank Loans in Ireland: How to Choose (2026)

The differences that actually matter — membership, speed, rates and flexibility · Rates referenced verified 22 July 2026

Credit unions and banks lend money in fundamentally different ways. A bank sells you a product; a credit union lends to you as a member-owner. That difference shapes everything — who can borrow, how fast the money arrives, how your application is judged, and what you pay. This guide walks through how the two models compare in Ireland so you can decide which route fits your situation. Access: the common bond is the first hurdle

The single biggest practical difference is that you cannot simply walk into a credit union and borrow. Irish credit unions operate on a common bond — membership is tied to where you live, work, or in some cases worship or study, and each credit union defines its own bond. Before a loan is possible you need to:

  1. Find a credit union whose bond you fall under — check ilcu.ie.
  2. Open a share (savings) account and make contributions — most credit unions want to see you save with them for a period before you borrow.
  3. Apply once you're an established member.

A bank or fintech, by contrast, will lend to any eligible applicant with no prior relationship. If you need money this week, that difference usually decides the question on its own.

Speed: banks win when time matters

Banks and fintechs can approve and pay out a personal loan within days. AIB advertises a 3-hour decision for existing customers. Revolut's loan is app-only with a near-instant decision. Because a credit union loan generally requires membership plus a savings record first, it is rarely the fast option — it rewards planning ahead, not urgency.

Price: banks are cheaper on average, but not always for you

On the headline numbers, banks win: the best standard bank rate in the market is PTSB at 7.20% APR, against a credit union national average of 10.42% APR (ILCU survey, July/August 2025). On €10,000 over 5 years, that gap is roughly €860 in extra interest.

But the average hides huge variation. Credit unions set rates individually, and many price by loan-size band. The best credit union rate we've confirmed — First Choice Credit Union at 8.84% APR — undercuts AIB's standard 8.95% APR. The legal ceiling for any Irish credit union is 12.68% APR. Your own credit union might sit anywhere in that range, which is why a personal quote beats any published average.

Assessment: the savings-record advantage

Most Irish credit unions check the Central Credit Register like any other lender — but they tend to take a broader view of individual circumstances, and they give real weight to your savings history with the credit union itself. A member who has saved steadily for years will often be offered better terms than a stranger with the same credit file. If your credit record has bumps but your savings habit is strong, a credit union may look at you more sympathetically than a bank's criteria allow.

Flexibility and extras on each side

Bank product features

  • Bank of Ireland lets you defer your first 3 repayments on its personal loan.
  • An Post Money charges no early repayment fee, and offers terms up to 120 months.
  • PTSB lends from €1,000 to €75,000 over up to 84 months.

Credit union member benefits

  • Possible dividends. Some credit unions pay an annual dividend on members' savings from their surplus. It's voted on yearly and can be zero — but banks offer nothing comparable.
  • Member-owned structure. A credit union answers to its members, not shareholders — surplus goes back into the institution and its community.
  • Preferential rates on some loan types. Some credit unions price car loans or home-improvement loans below their standard rate.

How to choose: a simple decision path

  1. Need the money within days? Bank or fintech. A credit union loan isn't built for urgency unless you're already an established member.
  2. Qualify for a green loan? If you have an approved SEAI grant, the SBCI energy upgrade loans (from 2.95% APR) beat everything else in the market — see the SEAI green loan eligibility guide →
  3. Already a credit union member with a savings record? Get their quote first — it may beat several banks, and the relationship counts in your favour.
  4. Neither? Start from the cheapest standard bank rates on our comparison table, then ask a credit union you're eligible to join whether it can do better.

Whatever you do, compare a real quote against a real quote — not an average against a headline rate. The credit union average tells you nothing about what your credit union will offer you personally.
