Irish Credit Card Benefits Ranked: Which Cards Give You the Most (2026)

Every benefit on every Irish credit card — cashback, 0% windows, insurance, rewards — sorted into tiers by real value. Verified from issuer websites and CCPC.ie, July 2026.

Irish credit cards are not famous for their perks. There is no Amex Platinum here, no points ecosystems, no airport-lounge arms race. What the Irish market does offer is a small set of concrete, countable benefits: two cashback offers, one travel insurance package, one airline rewards card, a charity card, and a spread of 0% introductory windows. This guide lists what every card on the market actually gives you, then ranks them into tiers by how much that benefit is realistically worth in euro.

One principle drives the ranking: a benefit is only worth what you'd otherwise have paid for it. Cashback is worth its face value. A 0% window is worth the interest you'd otherwise pay. Travel insurance is worth the policy you'd otherwise buy. Rewards you wouldn't use are worth nothing — whatever the brochure says.

Tier 1 — The benefits worth real money

Three cards offer benefits you can put a euro figure on with confidence. Which one tops the list depends on your situation — carrying debt, or spending and clearing in full.

Avant Money One Card — the only card that hands you cash for switching

Transfer a balance of €1,000 or more within 90 days of opening and Avant pays you €150 cashback — on top of 0% interest on that balance for 9 months, and 0% on purchases for the first 3 months. No annual fee. For anyone with existing card debt, this is the single most concrete benefit on the Irish market: cash in hand plus nine months of interest relief. The catch is the deadline — miss the 90-day transfer window and both the cashback and the 0% rate are gone.

An Post Money Classic — the biggest interest saving in Ireland

No cashback, no rewards — but 0% on balance transfers for 12 months, the longest window in the Irish market. On a typical €3,000 balance that would otherwise sit at 22.9% APR, that's several hundred euro in interest avoided over the year — comfortably more than any cashback offer pays. If your transferred balance is large or will take close to a year to clear, the extra three months over Avant's window is likely worth more than Avant's €150. Transfer within 90 days of opening. Full comparison in our balance transfer guide.

AIB Platinum Visa — the only ongoing cashback in Ireland

The one Irish card that pays you back year after year: 0.5% cashback on annual spend between €5,000 and €50,000, capped at €225 a year, with no annual fee. Spend €20,000 a year through the card and you collect €100 — every year, for doing nothing differently. Add up to 56 days interest-free on purchases and a 12-month 3.83% intro rate on purchases and balance transfers (transfers capped at €5,000). Two gates: you need a minimum €40,000 salary to qualify, and the cashback only starts once you've spent €5,000 in the year. This is the best card in Ireland for someone who spends heavily and clears the balance in full each month.

Tier 2 — Valuable, if the card fits your life

These benefits are real, but their value depends entirely on whether you'd have used or paid for the thing anyway.

An Post Money Flex — the spender's toolkit

0% on purchases for 9 months — Ireland's longest purchase window — plus instalment plans from 8.6% APR on purchases over €250, money transfers at 5.9% for 24 months, and the market's second-lowest standard APR at 15.7%. There's no headline giveaway here, but for someone planning a big purchase who needs time to pay it off, nine interest-free months is worth more than most cashback offers. The whole package is built around flexible repayment rather than perks.

BOI Platinum Advantage — travel insurance instead of a premium

Worldwide travel insurance for a €76.18 annual fee, plus a new-customer choice of 0% balance transfers for 7 months or 0% purchases for 6 months. The policy covers the cardholder, an authorised user (if any), and their partner and accompanying children (under 18, or under 23 in full-time education) — so it's genuinely a family policy, not cardholder-only — provided all insured people are under 81 and Irish-resident, with a €75 excess per person on most claim types. The maths is simple: if you currently buy annual multi-trip insurance for a similar group, the card may effectively pay for itself; if you rarely travel, you're paying €76.18 a year for cover you don't use. Requires a €40,000 minimum salary. Check the policy's cover levels and exclusions against your current insurance before switching.

BOI Aer Credit Card — for regular Aer Lingus flyers only

Ireland's only airline rewards card: Aer Lingus / AerClub rewards for €78 a year, billed at €6.50 a month, plus the same new-customer 0% choice as BOI's other cards. Spend €5,000 or more on the card in a year and you get 2 return flights to Europe (taxes/fees/charges still payable), plus 2 Aer Lingus lounge passes and 2 fast-track/priority boarding passes per reward year, and Avios earn on both Aer Lingus spend (100 per €100, plus a 300 Avios bonus from Aer Lingus) and everyday spend (25 per €100). BOI values the combined package at over €400 a year. Whether it's worth it comes down to one question: do you fly Aer Lingus often enough — and spend enough on the card — for the rewards to beat €78 in cash every year? For frequent flyers the answer can be yes; for everyone else, the fee buys perks that expire unused.

Revolut Credit Card — speed and points

Up to 6 months at 0% on both balance transfers and purchases, RevPoints on spending, no annual fee, and an app-only application with a near-instant decision — with a credit limit up to €10,000. The intro window is shorter than An Post's or Avant's and the duration can vary by offer, so confirm your exact terms in-app. Watch the 19.99% cash-advance APR. Best suited to existing Revolut users who value RevPoints and speed over the longest possible 0% window.

Tier 3 — Modest benefits

Solid cards with respectable intro offers, but nothing beyond them.

Bank of Ireland Classic

New customers choose either 0% on balance transfers for 7 months or 0% on purchases for 6 months — then it's a standard card at 22.1% APR with no fee and no ongoing perks. The either/or is the trap: you can't combine the two offers, so decide before applying which one you actually need.

PTSB Ice Visa

The only card besides Avant's that stacks both intro offers: 0% on balance transfers for 6 months plus 0% on purchases for 3 months, no fee. Shorter windows than the Tier 1 cards, but with no either/or choice and no new-customer restriction noted, it's a straightforward middle option. One balance transfer per card.

Bank of Ireland Affinity

The feel-good pick: charity-linked cards where the issuer supports the linked cause, plus a 2.9% fixed rate on balance transfers for 12 months — not 0%, but the reduced rate runs for a full year with no new-customer either/or. If supporting the charity matters to you, that's the benefit; just know that donating directly is almost always more efficient than donating via card scheme.

Tier 4 — No perks: the rate is the benefit

AIB Click Visa

No cashback, no rewards, no intro offer — and Ireland's lowest standard APR at 13.8%. If you ever carry a balance from month to month, this beats every rewards card in this guide: the interest saved against a typical 22.9% card exceeds any Irish cashback or rewards scheme at even modest balances. Online-only application, no fee. Boring is the benefit.

AIB 'be'

A 12-month 3.83% intro rate on purchases and balance transfers (transfers capped at €5,000) is the only offer; after that it's a two-tier purchase rate at 22.9% APR with no perks. Hard to recommend over Click for rate-seekers or over Platinum for AIB customers who qualify — 'be' sits in the middle with the benefits of neither.

How we ranked these

We valued each benefit at what it would otherwise cost you:

  • Cashback at face value — €150 from Avant is €150; AIB Platinum's cap is €225/year but requires €45,000+ of annual spend to max out.
  • 0% windows at the interest avoided — which scales with your balance, so long windows on big balances beat any cashback.
  • Insurance and rewards at replacement cost — the price of the policy or flights you'd have bought anyway, minus the annual fee. If you wouldn't have bought them, the value is zero.
  • Low APRs as a benefit in their own right for anyone who carries a balance — usually the biggest one of all.
