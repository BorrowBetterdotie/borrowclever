Budget 2027, Pension Rises and a Possible PTSB Bid: This Week's Money News

Budget 2027State PensionECBPTSBCost of Living

Budget 2027 talks dominate the agenda as household finances stay in focus

With Budget 2027 due next Tuesday, the week's news has been dominated by the horse-trading between Government ministers over how to spend the State's resources. RTÉ reports that a €10 rise in the State pension and core welfare payments looks likely to be agreed, which would be welcome news for households still feeling the pinch of the cost of living. Taoiseach Micheál Martin has pushed back on suggestions the negotiations have been "brutal", telling reporters the process between Ministers Simon Harris and Jack Chambers has actually been smoother than expected — a dynamic explored in more detail by The Irish Times.

Not everyone is happy with the shape of the Budget, however. People Before Profit has put forward its own alternative package, centred on a tax on millionaires, a higher corporation tax rate, an energy credit and a cap on fuel prices, aimed squarely at easing cost-of-living pressures — details are covered here. Whatever the final package looks like, it's worth remembering that budget measures like welfare increases and tax credits can directly affect household budgeting, which in turn shapes how much borrowing — on credit cards, personal loans or otherwise — people may need to manage day-to-day costs.

Economic backdrop: resilient but watching rate signals

Away from the Budget, the broader economic picture remains mixed. Bank of Ireland has given an upbeat assessment of the domestic economy despite international headwinds, a story explored by the Irish Times in the context of Ireland's unusually strong tax base compared with other heavily indebted economies. Meanwhile, investor group Barings is reportedly betting on softer European Central Bank rate moves ahead as part of its thesis on Irish rental assets, according to this report — a reminder that expectations around where ECB policy goes next continue to shift market sentiment.

In the US, a much weaker-than-expected jobs report for September has led traders to pare back bets on further Federal Reserve rate increases, as covered here. While the Fed and ECB are separate institutions, moves in US rate expectations often ripple into European borrowing costs and sentiment, so it's one worth keeping an eye on if you're considering a loan or mortgage in the months ahead.

Movement in Irish banking

On the banking front, Axis Capital, led by former banker Mark Duffy, is said to be considering a bid for PTSB, though the company has yet to secure funding for any potential offer, according to the Irish Times. It's early days, and nothing has been confirmed, but any ownership change at a retail bank is worth watching for borrowers and savers alike as it could eventually influence product offerings and competition in the market.
