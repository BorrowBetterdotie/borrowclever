Budget 2027, Bank Battles and a Resilient Economy: What It Means for Your Money

Budget 2027ECBPTSBInterest RatesBanking

A busy week for Irish money news

Between Budget 2027 preparations, a live takeover drama at one of the State's main retail banks, and fresh signals from the European Central Bank, it's been a packed few days for anyone trying to keep track of where Irish household finances are headed.

Budget 2027: childcare, tax and a €7 billion package

The Government is preparing an overall spending package of around €7 billion, with roughly €1.5 billion earmarked for tax reductions, according to early details reported by the Irish Times. Families are likely to be among the main beneficiaries, with improved childcare supports expected to extend to after-school care, potentially saving parents with a young child in creche around €1,000 a year. An income-tax package for workers is also expected. If you want to follow the announcement as it happens, the Irish Times has published a full schedule for Budget day, including live coverage and a Q&A.

Banking shake-up: PTSB and the Bawag question

Elsewhere, PTSB has found itself at the centre of takeover speculation. The bank said it had received no formal offer from corporate finance firm Axis Capital, describing a prior Axis statement as "highly speculative and conditional." PTSB says it remains focused on an existing offer from Bawag, as confirmed in further reporting from the Irish Times. For PTSB customers, there's no immediate change to accounts, mortgages or cards — but it's a situation worth watching, since ownership changes at a lender can eventually influence product ranges and customer terms over time.

Meanwhile, fintech challenger Revolut continues its rapid ascent, reportedly becoming Europe's most valuable startup just over a decade after launching as a cheaper alternative for foreign exchange. Its growth is a reminder that the competitive landscape for everyday banking and spending products in Ireland keeps shifting.

What's happening with interest rates?

On the wider economic backdrop, euro zone business activity has been expanding at its fastest pace in several years, according to the latest PMI survey, with Ireland's own services sector also showing strength despite ongoing price pressures, per the Irish Times. On the policy side, ECB chief economist Philip Lane suggested that drags on growth, including rising energy costs and higher yields, may limit how much further the ECB needs to tighten policy. For anyone with a tracker or variable-rate mortgage, or who is shopping around for a personal loan, this suggests the path for borrowing costs may be more stable than previously feared — though nothing is guaranteed.
