ECB Rate Path in Focus as Makhlouf Weighs Succession Talk and Hike Pressure

ECBInterest RatesCentral Bank of IrelandEuro Zone EconomyMortgages

Rate watchers get plenty to chew on

It's been a busy week for anyone tracking where euro zone interest rates are headed, with Central Bank of Ireland governor Gabriel Makhlouf at the centre of several strands of news. In his latest blog post, Makhlouf confirmed that the ECB Governing Council opted to leave its policy rate unchanged at its most recent meeting, while also sharing reflections from the IMF's Annual Meetings in Bangkok. Notably, separate reporting revealed that Makhlouf had personally considered backing a rate increase at that same meeting — a sign that, even with rates on hold for now, there's live debate within the Governing Council about whether borrowing costs need to move upward from here.

For Irish households with mortgages, personal loans, or credit card balances, that's a useful signal: the pause doesn't mean the door is closed on further tightening. Anyone with a variable-rate product, or an application for new credit in train, should keep an eye on ECB commentary over the coming weeks rather than assume rates have settled for good.

A possible changing of the guard at the ECB

The same Irish Times report also floated Makhlouf's view that Philip Lane — the ECB's current chief economist and a former Central Bank of Ireland governor himself — would make a strong candidate to eventually succeed Christine Lagarde. While this is speculative and a long way from any formal process, it's a reminder that ECB leadership and its approach to inflation and growth trade-offs could look quite different in the coming years, with real implications for how aggressively or cautiously future rate decisions are made.

Economy still outperforming expectations

Against this backdrop, the euro zone economy continues to show surprising strength. New figures show growth in the third quarter beat forecasts for a second consecutive period, even as households and businesses contend with elevated energy costs and the lingering effects of higher borrowing costs. Resilient growth generally gives central banks more room to consider raising rates if inflation remains sticky, since the economy appears able to absorb tighter conditions without stalling — another reason the "rates on hold" story may not be the final word.

Behind the scenes: financial system resilience

Separately, the Central Bank of Ireland used its fifth annual Financial Systems Conference in Croke Park to bring together around 500 policymakers and industry figures to discuss topics including geopolitical risk, European financial markets integration, and central bank digital currencies. While less directly relevant to everyday borrowing decisions, it's a useful backdrop showing regulators are actively thinking about how resilient the system needs to be as rate and growth dynamics evolve.

What it means for borrowers

Taken together, this week's news suggests the direction of travel for interest rates is still genuinely uncertain, with hawkish voices on the Governing Council and robust growth data both pointing toward the possibility of further increases rather than cuts in the near term. If you're comparing loan or credit card options at the moment, it may be worth locking in terms sooner rather than later, and paying close attention to whether any product you're considering is fixed or variable.
