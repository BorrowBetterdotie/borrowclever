Rates in Focus, Revolut Under Fire: What This Week's News Means for Irish Borrowers

A mixed week for rates, banking trust and housing supply

It's been a busy few days for anyone keeping an eye on the cost of borrowing, the safety of their banking data, and the wider health of the Irish mortgage and lending market. None of these stories exist in isolation — together they paint a picture of a financial system still finding its footing after several turbulent years, with implications for anyone holding a loan, credit card, or considering a new application.

Inflation eases, but central banks stay cautious

Revised figures show euro zone inflation in August came in lower than first estimated, a modest bit of good news for households across the currency bloc, Ireland included. But don't expect the European Central Bank to suddenly declare victory. Elsewhere, the picture is less settled: the Bank of England held rates steady while warning inflation could climb well above target next year, and the US Federal Reserve moved to raise rates for the first time in three years, flagging further increases ahead. For Irish borrowers, the takeaway is that the direction of travel for global interest rates remains uncertain, and anyone with a variable-rate mortgage, personal loan, or credit card balance should keep an eye on how ECB policy responds in the months ahead rather than assume rates have peaked or bottomed out.

Housing supply shows signs of life

On a brighter note, CSO figures show a sharp jump in planning permissions granted in the second quarter compared with the same period last year. More homes in the pipeline could eventually ease pressure on both the property market and mortgage demand, though it will be some time before this translates into homes ready for buyers or renters.

Revolut faces a serious security scare

Fintech users should take note of developments at Revolut, where a hacking group is reportedly demanding a multimillion-dollar ransom after claiming to obtain confidential customer records, threatening to sell the data if payment isn't made. This follows an earlier report that the same group spent months posing as law enforcement to target high-value Revolut customers. Anyone using digital banking apps should stay alert for phishing attempts and follow official guidance from their provider.

Tracker mortgage saga takes another turn

In a significant legal development, former PTSB chief executive David Guinane has won his appeal against a Central Bank sanction and fine related to the long-running tracker mortgage scandal, with a tribunal overturning the finding against him. The case is a reminder that the fallout from the tracker mortgage controversy is still working its way through Ireland's regulatory and legal system years later.
