Budget 2027, Rising Bills and a Possible Pension Boost: What It Means for Your Wallet

Budget 2027 shapes up amid a squeeze on household budgets

With Budget 2027 on the horizon, the political conversation is already turning into a battle over framing — how the Government presents its choices versus how households actually feel them in their pockets. As RTÉ's analysis of the budget politics notes, the numbers only tell part of the story; the narrative around them often matters just as much to voters trying to work out whether they're better or worse off.

Six months of rising costs

That narrative is being tested against some uncomfortable arithmetic. RTÉ Business has been tracking how much more consumers are paying for energy, fuel and mortgage repayments since the US began launching strikes on Iran at the end of February. For anyone with a variable-rate mortgage, a personal loan, or a credit card balance ticking over, this period has served as a reminder of how quickly external shocks can filter through to everyday repayments. Energy and fuel costs feeding into general inflation tend to keep pressure on the European Central Bank's rate outlook, and further ECB moves — in whichever direction — will continue to influence what banks and lenders charge on mortgages, loans and cards here in Ireland.

For households already stretched, this is a useful moment to review existing borrowing. Anyone carrying credit card debt or a personal loan taken out before this period of volatility may find it worthwhile to check whether a better deal is available elsewhere, particularly if their current rate was set before recent market movements.

A possible boost for pensioners

On the other side of the ledger, there's some potentially welcome news for older households. The Government is reportedly considering raising the weekly State pension by €7.50 as part of Budget 2027. If confirmed, this would provide some offset to the rising cost of living outlined above, though it remains to be seen how far it stretches against higher energy, fuel and repayment costs in the months ahead.

What this means for borrowers

Taken together, these stories point to a Budget 2027 landscape shaped heavily by cost-of-living pressures. Whether you're a mortgage holder watching repayments creep up, a pensioner awaiting confirmation of a rate increase, or simply someone trying to plan around energy and fuel costs, it's worth keeping an eye on how the final Budget package addresses — or fails to address — the squeeze many households have felt since the start of the year.
