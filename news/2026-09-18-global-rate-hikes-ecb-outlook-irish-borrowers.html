Rate Watch: Global Central Banks Turn Hawkish as Irish Borrowers Face a Bumpier Road Ahead

A hawkish mood is spreading through global central banking

It's been a busy few days for interest rate watchers. The Bank of Japan lifted its rates to their highest level in 31 years, with the Irish Times noting this brings Japan's benchmark to a level not seen since 1995. The move came as global markets were still digesting news of a fresh US rate hike, which sparked a stock rally even as it signalled that major economies remain focused on taming inflation rather than easing borrowing costs.

Closer to home, European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde was notably non-committal on what comes next. She told reporters the ECB would decide "meeting by meeting", and that any further moves would depend on how the economic picture develops. For anyone with a mortgage, personal loan, or credit card balance in Ireland, that's a signal worth paying attention to: after a run of global tightening, there's no clear indication the ECB is finished raising rates either. Borrowers on variable-rate products, or anyone weighing up a new loan, should factor in the possibility that borrowing costs could move higher yet before they move lower.

Irish banks keep shoring up funding

Against that backdrop, Bank of Ireland completed its fourth bond sale of the year, raising funds through a green Tier 2 bond. Moves like this are part of routine funding management for large lenders and shouldn't be read as a signal about retail rates one way or the other — but it's a reminder that funding costs for banks are also shifting in this environment, which can eventually feed through to what's on offer for savers and borrowers.

Inflation isn't hitting everyone equally

New research from the CSO, covered by the Irish Times, found that renters and younger households are feeling the pinch from inflation more acutely than other groups. That's a useful reminder that broad economic trends — rate decisions, inflation prints, bond sales — don't land evenly. If you're renting and juggling credit card repayments or considering a personal loan to smooth out costs, it's worth shopping around rather than assuming a one-size-fits-all deal works for your situation.

Elsewhere in Irish finance

On a different note, Revolut's founder confirmed the fintech is weighing a dual listing in London and New York, while Central Bank of Ireland Governor Gabriel Makhlouf used back-to-back speeches this week to press the case for building trust into financial innovation and for speeding up completion of the EU Single Market. Meanwhile, planning permissions for new homes jumped sharply in the second quarter, according to the CSO — a potentially encouraging sign for future housing supply, even if it won't ease borrowing costs in the short term.
