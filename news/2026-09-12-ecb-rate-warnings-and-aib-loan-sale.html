Rate Hikes Loom Larger as ECB Signals More Pain Ahead for Borrowers

ECB keeps door open for more rate hikes as inflation bites

Irish borrowers watching their loan and credit card repayments got a fresh reminder this week that the era of rock-bottom interest rates isn't returning any time soon. The European Central Bank's chief economist, Philip Lane warned that the inflation squeeze will worsen, describing the latest rate move as a "measured adjustment" to what he called a significant inflation issue. Crucially, he left the door open to further hikes if price pressures don't ease.

Central Bank of Ireland governor Gabriel Makhlouf struck a similarly cautious tone, declining to rule future increases in or out. His message was clear: the path for rates isn't fixed, but inflation remains a serious concern for policymakers. For anyone with a variable-rate mortgage, personal loan, or revolving credit card balance, that ambiguity is worth paying attention to — it suggests borrowing costs are more likely to rise further than fall in the near term.

Adding to the picture, US consumer inflation picked up in August, driven partly by a rebound in petrol prices. That's fuelled market expectations the Federal Reserve could move on rates too. While the Fed's decisions don't directly set Irish rates, synchronised inflation pressure across major economies tends to reinforce the ECB's own inclination to stay tight rather than loosen policy early.

AIB moves to offload problem personal loans

Closer to home, AIB is preparing to sell a portfolio of under €100 million, made up mainly of problem personal loans, as the bank also works to hire its next chief financial officer. Loan sales like this are a fairly routine part of how banks manage their balance sheets, but they're a useful reminder for consumers: if you're carrying a personal loan that's fallen into arrears, it can end up being sold on to a different loan owner or collections agency, often with different contact terms. If you're struggling with repayments, it's worth engaging with your lender early rather than waiting to see who ends up holding the debt.

What it means for anyone borrowing right now

Between hawkish signals from the ECB and Central Bank, and inflation surprising to the upside in the US, the overall direction of travel for interest rates looks firmer for longer rather than easing off soon. If you're shopping around for a personal loan or comparing credit cards, it's worth checking whether you'd be better off locking in current terms now rather than waiting, and comparing offers across several lenders rather than settling for the first one you find.

On a lighter note, AIB also published a report on the economic ripple effect of Katie Taylor's farewell fight at Croke Park, estimating close to half a million euro was spent in pubs and restaurants in Dublin's north inner city on the night — a reminder that even amid rate anxiety, big local events can give a real short-term boost to small businesses.
