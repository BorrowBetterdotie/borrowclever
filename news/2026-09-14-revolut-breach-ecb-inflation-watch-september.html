Data Breaches, Scam Warnings and Inflation Worries: This Week's Money Roundup

Data BreachScamsECBPensions

Security worries dominate the week's personal finance news

It's been a week where consumer vigilance has taken centre stage, with borrowers and savers alike being reminded that scammers are getting more sophisticated — and that the wider economic backdrop remains unsettled.

Revolut data breach exposes sensitive customer details

Revolut has confirmed that some customers had passports, driving licences, dates of birth, home addresses, emails and phone numbers exposed after criminals used fake government requests in a "sophisticated impersonation scam", according to the Irish Times. RTE reports that a limited number of accounts were affected, but the type of data involved means those impacted should be alert to follow-on identity theft or phishing attempts. If you bank or hold cards with any provider, it's worth reviewing the Irish Times' practical guide on what to do if you're caught up in a data breach, including monitoring statements and being wary of unexpected calls or emails referencing your account.

Central Bank flags another unauthorised clone firm

Separately, the Central Bank of Ireland has issued a fresh warning about an unauthorised entity trading as "FPPE Fund plc (CLONE)", which is impersonating a legitimate business using cloned websites, email addresses and phone numbers to target consumers. Anyone contacted by unfamiliar investment or loan "providers" should independently verify authorisation on the Central Bank's register before sharing any personal or financial details.

ECB attention turns to gas prices and inflation

On the wider rates picture, ECB policymaker Peter Kazimir has said euro zone inflation could run higher than current projections, with gas and power prices now a key watch point. That comes as European capitals are reportedly negotiating a "grand package" over who will succeed Christine Lagarde and fill other senior ECB roles, adding a layer of political uncertainty to future rate decisions. Meanwhile in the US, President Trump has repeated his view that America should have the world's lowest interest rates, ahead of the Federal Reserve's next meeting — a reminder that borrowing costs here can be influenced by global as well as domestic pressures.

A brighter note: pension coverage climbs

Not all the news was cautionary. New CCPC-backed research shows pension ownership has risen sharply since the launch of auto-enrolment, suggesting more workers are building long-term savings habits even amid cost-of-living pressures.
